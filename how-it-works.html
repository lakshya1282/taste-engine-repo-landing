How Taste Engine works.

01

Brief.

You give an idea and an audience. The skill pulls out what it is, who it's for, the feeling in the first 3 seconds, the one goal and what it must never look like. It asks at most one question.

02

Dice.

A terminal script rolls everything that usually defaults to "safe": which gallery and position to take the seed from, which 3 of 5 things to inherit, background lightness, palette logic, type personality, layout skeleton, a motion signature, an extra constraint and an unrelated "wildcard world" to borrow from. The agent doesn't get to reroll because it dislikes a result.

03

Seed crawl.

The agent opens the picked site like a visitor. It scrolls every page, hovers every interactive thing, and runs small snippets to read the real fonts, colors, CSS variables, keyframes, transitions and scroll settings. It is polite: it reads robots.txt, waits between requests, never logs in and never bypasses a block.

04

design.md.

Everything it measured goes into one document: color roles and ratios, type table, layout and spacing, components, a motion catalog (trigger, target, from to, duration, ease), the interaction list, media technique, tech stack, why it works, and a "never copy" list. If something can't be measured, it says so.

05

Reference scan.

Four to six extra sites calibrate its judgment: at least two from unrelated worlds and one weird one. This is how it notices what's overused right now.

06

Concept.

Before any visual decision, the agent writes five big ideas for your site and deletes the first two, because those are the obvious ones. Then it states one sentence: "This site is a ___ that ___." The seed gives craft, never the idea.

07

Direction card and mutation plan.

The dice act as mutation operators. Three anchors from the seed are kept nearly intact (for example motion, type hierarchy and spacing). Everything else must change: layout order, background, palette hues, at least one font in the pairing, and the interactions' look.

08

Clean-room build.

The agent builds from design.md and the tokens, never from the seed's code. It follows the layout skeleton and adds the rolled motion signature. It keeps the first screen to five elements or fewer, bans fake stats, fake clocks and filler labels, and previews in the browser at desktop and mobile widths.

09

Gates.

Three rigorous checks before it ships:

  • Similarity gate: At least 4 of 6 dimensions must clearly differ from the seed (concept, section structure, dominant color, type pairing, media, signature interaction).
  • Slop scan: A list of first- and second-generation AI design clichés. Each hit costs a point.
  • Parity test: Your screenshots next to real winners. Could it sit in the same gallery row?

Then a score out of 40. Pass means 32 or more, nothing below 3, no slop hits. It gets two revision loops, then reports honestly if it still falls short.

10

Delivery.

You get the site, the design files, the seed credit, the dice results and a list of placeholders to replace.

SEED EVOLUTION

Inherit vs Mutate.

Always Ours Inherited from the seed (3 of 5, rolled) Always Changed
Concept, copy, media, logo and icons Motion language, color relationships, type hierarchy, spacing rhythm, interaction mechanics Section order, background, at least one font, dominant color
Drawn strictly from the user's brief, burned ideas, and custom illustrations/schematics. Exact cubic-bezier values, staggers, and ratio contrasts measured from the seed. Guarantees the resulting website has a completely independent visual identity.

Why dice?

Left alone, models drift toward the most probable choice. Dice make the improbable choice happen. Forced constraints are what make two runs different.

Why a clean room?

Ideas and techniques can be learned. Someone else's code, words and images can't be taken. The build step only reads the design document, never the seed's source.
