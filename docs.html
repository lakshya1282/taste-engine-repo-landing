Taste Engine Documentation

A calm and complete guide to using the skill, understanding outputs, and configuring dice.

1. Quick start

Get up and running with Taste Engine in less than two minutes:

  1. Install the skill: Put the SKILL.md file into your agent's skills directory (see Install).
  2. Open a project folder in your coding agent workspace.
  3. Give it a brief:
    TERMINAL / CHAT PROMPT 
    /taste-engine design a landing page for a thrift-resale app for college students in India

    Or in plain words: "Use taste-engine to redesign my portfolio. Audience: startup founders hiring designers."

  4. Let it run: It will display the Direction Card, execute the seed crawl, build the clean-room site, preview it, and report back with scorecard metrics.

2. What to put in your brief

The skill extracts exactly what it needs from your prompt to avoid creative drift:

  • What it is: Product, service, portfolio, campaign, or dev tool.
  • Who it’s for: Age, culture, where they hang out online, and what aesthetic they already love.
  • The feeling in the first 3 seconds: Visceral adjective or phrase (e.g. "tactile and loud").
  • The one action: The single primary conversion goal for the visitor.
  • What it must never look like: The specific tropes you want strictly forbidden.
  • Your stack: Defaults to plain HTML + CSS + JS with GSAP and Lenis unless named otherwise.
💡 Pro-Tip on Luxury: "Luxury", "premium", "serious", or "B2B" does not mean dark + gold + serif. It means confidence and restraint, which can be expressed in a hundred other ways. Say what you actually mean.

3. What it creates

Every Taste Engine execution generates an audit trail under your-project/design/:

File Description
design/brief.md Your initial brief, cleaned up, with assumptions and audience profile.
design/dice.md The exact terminal dice results that govern mutations.
design/design.md The seed site’s measured design document (colors, type, motion catalog).
design/references.md Notes and craft teardown from 4–6 additional reference sites.
design/concept.md Five generated big ideas, two deleted, one chosen.
design/direction.md The Direction Card, mutation plan, and signature moves.
design/tokens.md Your site’s complete CSS custom properties design system.
design/critique.md Gate results, scorecard out of 40, and revision logs.
design/refs/ Private study screenshots and extracted text. Git-ignored. Never shipped.

4. The dice

The dice prevent creative collapse by forcing unpredictable aesthetic choices:

Die What it controls
Seed source Which gallery, listing page, and position the seed comes from.
Inherit anchors Which 3 of 5 design elements to take nearly intact from the seed.
Background Light paper, saturated color, mid-tone, chromatic dark, split screen, etc.
Palette logic Physical object colors, clashing hues, opposite accent, fluorescent on neutrals.
Type personality Quirky grotesk, wedge serif, condensed poster, variable font play, marker/handwritten.
Layout skeleton Single viewport, horizontal scroll, pinned scene, interactive index, draggable canvas.
Motion signature Velocity skew, shape masks, physics, cursor character, WebGL distortion, color floods.
Extra constraint No cards, no top nav, something to throw, max 4 elements in first screen.
Wildcard world An unrelated industry (fashion, indie game, zine studio, packaging) to borrow from.

The full lists live inside SKILL.md. You can edit them to add custom faces.

5. Inherit and mutate rules

How the skill balances craft inheritance with total originality:

  • Always new: Concept, copy, media, logo, and icons are never copied from the seed.
  • Always replaced: Section order (dictated by the layout skeleton roll) and background (dictated by the background roll).
  • Anchors (3 of 5): Kept nearly intact. For color relationships, you may keep up to 3 exact hex values, but the dominant surface or accent must change. For type hierarchy, maintain the scale ratio and size relationships, but change at least one family in the pairing.
  • Non-anchors: Must change in kind to ensure divergence.

6. Gates and scoring

Every generated site must pass three gates before shipping:

  • Similarity gate: At least 4 of 6 dimensions must be clearly different from the seed (concept, structure, dominant color, type pairing, media, interaction).
  • Slop scan: Each cliché hit costs -1 point. Zero hits required to pass.
  • Parity test: Side-by-side comparison with 3 real references. Could it sit in the same gallery row?
  • Scorecard: Eight categories scored 1 to 5. Passing threshold is 32/40 or higher, with no single category below 3. Maximum of two revision loops.

7. Safety and etiquette

Taste Engine crawls with extreme respect for web infrastructure:

  • Always reads and respects /robots.txt before any request.
  • Waits at least 1 second between requests and caps total requests at ~30 per site.
  • Immediately halts on HTTP 403 or 429.
  • Never logs in, and never bypasses a login wall, captcha, or bot challenge.
  • Saves only text (HTML, CSS, JS) for measurement. Never downloads proprietary video, images, or font files.
  • Always credits the seed site in the delivery note.

8. Customizing

You can fine-tune Taste Engine for your team's workflow:

  • Edit dice faces: Open SKILL.md and modify the Python dice list in Phase 2.
  • Ban new cliches: Add emerging tropes to the Phase 9 slop fingerprint list.
  • Change default stack: Specify Next.js, React, Astro, or Tailwind instead of plain HTML/CSS/JS.
  • Raise standards: Increase the scorecard passing bar to 36/40.

9. Troubleshooting

The skill doesn't show up: Ensure the path is exactly skills/taste-engine/SKILL.md (one level deep) and restart the conversation session.
It skipped the crawl: Prompt your agent: "Follow the taste-engine phases in order. Start with the dice and the seed crawl."
It can't run console snippets: The agent automatically falls back to reading raw stylesheets and marks data as "inferred".
The seed was blocked: Completely normal. It logs the skip and takes the next position on the page.
Result looks too close to the seed: Ask the agent to mutate dominant color, structure, and type pairing, then re-evaluate the similarity gate.
